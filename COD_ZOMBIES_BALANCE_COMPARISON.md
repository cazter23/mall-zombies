# BO1–Cold War Zombies balance comparison for Mall Zombies

## Evidence rules

This comparison uses the supplied local script collections only. It does not fill gaps from wikis, memory, or retail guides.

- **Direct**: the value or formula is visible in an active-looking script or reconstructed engine path.
- **Map-specific**: direct, but valid only for the named map or archetype.
- **Derived**: arithmetic from a direct formula.
- **Asset missing**: the script reads a table, behavior bundle, weapon definition, or map entity that is not in the dump.
- **Legacy/unused risk**: readable code exists, but a newer caller uses a different path or no active caller was established.

The BO1 source is reconstructed engine code plus community mods, not a complete retail GSC and asset dump. Its exact round, economy, and gun values are mostly absent ([BO1 provenance](references/bo1-zombies-decompiled-main/README.md#L3), [external retail assets](references/bo1-zombies-decompiled-main/setup.ps1#L5)). BO3 is the most numerically complete traditional round-based set. BO4 omits its difficulty bundle and ordinary weapon assets. Cold War contains multiple patch variants and moves many decisive values into settings, behavior, score, and item assets. “Unknown” below means the supplied evidence cannot support an exact number.

Values deliberately excluded from the comparison:

| Visible value | Classification | Why it is not retail balance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| BO1 `horde_max_zombies`, carryover rounds, Nightmare respawn timers | **Community mod** | Each module explicitly says it is not part of the original game. It is useful only as a stress experiment. |
| BO3 `zombie_default_max` override inside `/# ... #/` | **Developer-only compiled-out block** | It is a test override around the real early-round reducer, not a shipped quota. |
| BO4 difficulty resolver’s zeroes when bundle fields are absent | **Defensive fallback / placeholder** | Zero is substituted for missing fields; it does not mean retail base HP, spawn delay, or count was zero. |
| Cold War `level.zombie_health ... : 100` | **Fallback** | It is used only when the cached behavior-derived health is absent ([fallback](references/t9-src-main/vm-38/scripts/zm_common/zm_round_logic.gsc#L317)). |
| Cold War shared 5,000/2,500/1,500 PaP helper | **Legacy path** | The traced modern purchase menu uses 5,000/15,000/30,000 instead. |

**Similarities.** BO3 and BO4 share the classic separation between round quota, concurrent actors, spawn cadence, health, and gait. Cold War retains that separation but data-drives more of it. All visible traditional paths treat special rounds as scheduled encounter changes rather than a permanent random percentage layered over every spawn.

**Evolution.** BO3’s health curve grows forever at 10%; BO4 adds a round-35 clamp for lower-difficulty classic play; Cold War replaces the single exponential tail with authored bands and a maximum. That is a clear move from “eventually everything becomes a sponge” toward “late-game durability is designed around the completed build.” Speed similarly evolves from three stochastic categories to an explicit super-sprint tier.

**Verdict for Mall Zombies.** Use BO3’s transparent early/mid-round structure, but use the Cold War idea for the completed-build phase: a slower final health band or cap after Platinum’s intended R24 window. Keep Mall’s gentler early live caps. Schedule special composition per round rather than relying entirely on independent rolls.

**Similarities.** The proven spawn model is “enabled and connected encounter network + active cap + successful-spawn accounting,” not unrestricted random points. BO1 stress tests and all later scripts treat stalled actors as bookkeeping problems that must preserve quota and health rather than give a free kill.

**Evolution.** Spawn cadence remains remarkably stable from BO3 through Cold War: 5% faster per round, floor .1 seconds, stop accelerating after R60. The larger changes are active population and composition: Cold War exposes a 32-enemy default, while BO4/CW increasingly pre-plan archetype counts rather than allowing pure random streaks.

**Verdict for Mall Zombies.** Its open-zone, finite-route, minimum-distance, body-clearance, reuse-cooldown, route-neighborhood selection is stronger and more readable than a raw random spawner. Keep 24 alive for now. Add BO4-style per-round special allocations and active Cart limits; add diagnostics for approaching/waiting/unreachable/rescued enemies before changing the cap.

## 3. Difficulty over time

| Phase | BO1 | BO3 | BO4 | Cold War |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early game | Exact curve missing. Classic structure emphasizes a weak starting weapon, door choices, and limited space. | R1–5 rapidly move from 6 to 24 total enemies while HP rises 150→550; spawn delay falls 2.00→1.63 s. Full 24 live cap is available immediately, though quota limits early rounds. | Same early-round fractional quota structure, with difficulty-controlled underlying values and more special-archetype systems. | External per-round encounter table can author early totals directly; loadout weapon and persistent skill assumptions make the baseline less comparable to a fresh weak pistol. |
| Midgame pressure | Missing exact values. | Sprint begins appearing around R10, HP becomes exponential at R10, count growth becomes roughly quadratic, and ordinary weapons increasingly require Pack-a-Punch/AAT. | More constrained special composition, explicit heavy limits, repeat damage upgrades, longer default 15 s shopping break. | Rarity, PaP tiers, perk acquisition, armor, ammo mods, and equipment scaling create several parallel power ladders. |
| Late-game viability | Classic exact tail unavailable; the engine supports penetration and locational weapon tuning. | No health cap in the visible default. Difficulty increasingly becomes ammunition/weapon-scaling endurance; AATs and wonder weapons keep late rounds practical. | Lower-difficulty `zclassic` can stop health growth at R35. Re-PaP damage can rise to 2× on the visible repeat path; powerful effects use cooldowns and class exceptions. | Banded HP and `maxhealth` support a designed plateau. Three PaP costs, rarity, ammo mods, armor, equipment scaling, perks, and optional exfil keep late play viable while making a completed build much stronger. |
| Where upgrades become mandatory | Cannot calculate without health/gun assets. | Exact round depends on the weapon asset, but the exponential R10+ curve guarantees that unupgraded fixed-damage guns lose relevance. | Exact point depends on missing base values; cap/repacks reduce the endless-sponge problem. | Exact point depends on missing weapon and health assets; the economy explicitly expects multiple PaP/rarity purchases. |

**Similarities.** Every game increases several axes—health, total workload, arrival rate, speed, and composition. None of the later games relies on health alone. Their upgrade systems are part of the difficulty curve, so “unupgraded gun becomes weak” is intentional; the important design question is whether the player can afford the next power step before it is required.

**Evolution.** BO3’s late game is open-ended attrition. BO4 partially reverses that through a difficulty-dependent health cap. Cold War commits further to a bounded durability curve but expands player progression and defenses to match. Difficulty shifts from raw zombie durability toward build planning, elites, super-sprinters, armor maintenance, and optional exit timing.

**Verdict for Mall Zombies.** The best fit is a BO3-like demanding midgame with a Cold War-like completed-build plateau. Platinum currently stops at a stated R24 target while Mall health keeps compounding forever. After roughly R24–30, slow or cap ordinary HP and let density, Cart/Walker composition, spawn pressure, and player mistakes carry difficulty.

**Evolution.** BO3 rewards weak guns for landing many bullets. BO4 reverses that incentive with a fixed damage-point budget per enemy. Cold War’s exact score events are missing, but its purchase economy escalates much more aggressively: PaP costs rise rather than fall, perks get dearer as slots fill, and ammo cost tracks upgrade state. BO4 repeat PaP becoming cheaper and Cold War later PaP becoming dramatically dearer are a direct reversal.

**Verdict for Mall Zombies.** BO4’s bounded damage-income model best prevents Price Gun from remaining economically dominant solely because it takes more bullets. Preserve the recognizable 60/100/130 finish ladder if desired, but cap damage points per normal or award them by health removed. Cold War’s principle—not its 20× magnitude—fits RESTOCK: price refills by gun/warranty value rather than one flat 400. Recalculate door timing after either change.

Current Price/Rivet values already put Rivet ahead in body DPS, head DPS, magazine damage, and carried damage; test net points and exposure before another stat change.

**Similarities.** A script-visible same-attacker grace period in BO3/BO4/CW is not global invulnerability; crowds remain lethal. Every later game assumes recovery systems beyond raw base health—downing, revive, perks, shields/armor, or self-revive.

**Verdict for Mall Zombies.** Keep the clean one-life, three-hit baseline, but recognize that copying later-game density without their armor/revive stack raises effective difficulty substantially. Compensate through readable telegraphs, special concurrency limits, far-only stuck rescue, and a completed-build health curve. If Wardrobe adds survival, prefer one explicit limited rescue or modest mitigation choice over passive armor becoming mandatory.

## Fundamentals supported across the series

No exact numerical constant is proven by all four supplied dumps because BO1 lacks its retail tuning scripts. The structural overlap is still strong:

1. **Separate quota, concurrent cap, cadence, health, and speed.** Each affects difficulty differently and should be tuned independently.
2. **Opening the map changes the spawn network.** A door buys space and services while potentially adding attack routes.
3. **Weapon balance is multidimensional.** Damage, headshots, cadence, range, penetration, magazine, reload, reserve, handling, price, and refill cost all matter.
4. **Upgrades are expected to answer rising health.** A fixed-damage starter is not supposed to remain effortless forever, but later games increasingly let investment preserve a preferred weapon.
5. **Special enemies need encounter rules.** Scheduling, live limits, health curves, weak points, and class interactions matter more than a raw spawn percentage.
6. **Rounds need closure and recovery beats.** Special rounds, shopping breaks, drops, and optional objectives vary the pressure.
7. **Anti-stall logic must preserve state.** A rescued/requeued enemy should retain health and reward bookkeeping and should not disappear from immediate combat as a free bailout.

## What changed, and why it matters

| Transition | Direction | Best explanation for the design change |
|---|---|---|
| BO1/BO3 classic damage points → BO4 bounded enemy pool | Weak-gun farming is constrained | Strong upgrades no longer destroy earning power as severely, and high-RPM weapons do not dominate income simply through hit count. |
| BO3 infinite 10% HP tail → BO4 conditional R35 clamp → CW bands/max | Late durability becomes bounded | Completed builds can remain useful; late difficulty moves toward composition, speed, and resource upkeep. |
| BO3 initial PaP + AAT → BO4 repeat raw-damage levels → CW rarity + three PaP tiers | More investment can follow one gun | Players retain weapon preference, but the economy gains more mandatory-looking ladders. |
| BO4 decreasing repeat costs → CW 5k/15k/30k | Later power becomes a much larger commitment | Cold War’s richer income/loadout/armor economy can support steeper sinks; this is a reversal, not a universal pricing rule. |
| Random/map-specific specials → BO4 quotas/live caps → CW encounter tables | Composition becomes authored | Larger rosters require protection from unreadable streaks and simultaneous disruptive enemies. |
| 24 classic live enemies → CW visible 32 default | More immediate density | Modern mobility, armor, equipment, field effects, and stronger build retention compensate for higher pressure. |
| Health/Jugg/revive → shields/fail-safe perks → armor/self-revive | More defensive layers | Later encounter density cannot be copied fairly without accounting for the player’s larger mistake budget. |
| Endless survival → optional exfil | Runs gain a successful endpoint | Players can convert a strong build into a final challenge instead of always ending in attrition. |

## Recommended Mall Zombies model

The best match is a deliberate hybrid:

- **BO3 for the core:** simple points, readable rounds, a 24-enemy target cap, finite sprint, strong headshot incentives, and escalating arrival pressure.
- **BO4 for control:** bounded damage income, planned special composition, explicit live limits, capped heavy health, longer shopping rhythm, and strict cooldown/ownership rules for Build-A-Blaster effects.
- **Cold War for the late game:** a final HP band or cap, upgrade-sensitive RESTOCK pricing, preserved per-gun investment, and eventually an optional extraction.
- **BO1 for combat texture:** meaningful range falloff, weapon-specific location damage, material penetration, and crowd movement that can wait/slow/dodge without ghosting near the player.

The immediate balance work this comparison supports is:

1. Keep the current Rivet/Price damage hierarchy and test **net income per round**, not damage alone.
2. Replace flat RESTOCK pricing with modest weapon/warranty bands or a partial-refill model.
3. Convert Walker/Cart random rolls into a shuffled per-round budget; cap simultaneous Cart threats.
4. Decide the post-Platinum health curve now: slower band, cap, or intended attrition endpoint.
5. Trial 8–10-second breaks or periodic longer shopping rounds.
6. Before Wardrobe and Build-A-Blaster, define combined-stat caps, proc ownership, recursion rules, elite reductions, and whether any one-life rescue exists.

Those choices preserve Mall Zombies’ faster, cleaner solo loop while adopting the parts of later Zombies balance that solve real scaling problems.
